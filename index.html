feat(ml): fraud score and approval policy

DFS Fraud Model

Wake Forest MSBA Analytics Challenge, Team Project

Context

Deacon Financial Services, a bank case, was losing money to bonus fraud during a new account promotion. Leadership asked if we could spot fraud at the point of application, and what they should do about it.

My role

Built one of the team's 35 models. My random forest tied for lowest expected cost per account. I wrote the supporting analysis and recommendation sections of our memos, helped build the deck, and presented the closing slides to faculty and industry professionals.

Outcome

Our recommended model, a random forest with network features (0.880 test AUC), stops 54% of fraud vs. 38% under the prior policy. That cuts expected cost per account by 24%, about $47,000 every 30 days.

Tools → Python · scikit-learn · CatBoost · SQL · DBeaver Approach → Logistic regression → 9 ML model types → random forest + network features

Field Notes

What I learned this semester, and how it changes the way I work.

Better data beats better algorithms

On the DFS case, five different algorithms landed within 0.004 AUC of each other once they had the same data. Adding network data (shared devices, phones, and payout accounts) moved AUC eight times more than switching models did. Now I ask what data is missing before I ask which model to use.

New data can look better than it is

In the same case, September fraud looked like 1.4% on October 1 and 5.1% a month later. Young accounts look safe because their fraud hasn't been caught yet. I check how mature the labels are before I trust any recent performance number.

Who pays when AI agents cause harm

In a position paper, I argued that liability for harm caused by agentic AI should be shared between the developer and the company using it. My partner argued the deploying company should carry most of it. In our combined paper her side led, and mine both challenged and supported it. It made me think harder about where human review belongs in any automated decision.

Where I'm going next

I want a data analyst role where I build both the analysis and the process behind it, like the Nexus Wizard and the DFS approval policy. A model is only useful once someone knows what to do with its output.
